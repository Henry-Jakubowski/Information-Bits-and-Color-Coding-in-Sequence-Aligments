The math behind the threshold

Treat each sequence's residue at this column as an independent draw from the real amino acid background (the same BLOSUM62-associated frequencies used throughout this chapter). The information content of the whole column is how surprising it would be, by pure chance, to see exactly this mix of N−k common residues and k rare ones:

—

Because each additional common-residue sequence adds a fixed number of bits, I climbs steadily as N grows while k stays fixed — it crosses any given threshold sooner for a rarer substitution and later for a common one. That's the whole mechanism: more sequences agreeing on the same residue makes it statistically less plausible that the one outlier is just noise.

This is a different question from the column-composition score used earlier for "Position 35" (S/G/G/S), which compares observed proportions to background frequencies and barely moves as N grows with a fixed minority share. Here we're asking how surprising the specific sequence of outcomes is — the same idea as the ordered S–G–G–S probability, just extended to a column with a majority and a minority residue instead of four distinct letters.
